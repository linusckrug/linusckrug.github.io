About Me

I am a PhD candidate in the Department of Sociology at the University of Zurich. In early 2027, I will join the Stanford Center on Poverty and Inequality (CPI) as a Postdoctoral Scholar.

My research examines how the organization of work and the institutional structure of labor markets shape social and economic mobility and stratification over the life course and across generations. In my dissertation, I show how occupations shape intergenerational mobility beyond class and status, how the institutional organization of work structures the formation of occupational aspirations, and how parents' labor market positions, beyond their economic and human capital, influence children's educational trajectories. To that end, I treat occupations not just as proxies for socioeconomic standing, but as institutionally and organizationally embedded positions that bundle distinctive resources and entail particular life worlds.

Across my work, I advance a relational account of work and mobility, and provide insights how the institutional structure of labor markets shapes the transmission of (dis)advantage across generations and channels opportunity over careers.

Drawing on large scale survey data and administrative records, I use quantitative and computational methods, including network analytic tools and simulation.

Prior to my PhD, I received an MSc in Socioeconomics from Vienna University of Economics and Business and studied at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill. During my doctorate, I was a visiting scholar at Stanford University.
